## Headline

The clearest, most repeated failure trigger in Glean's own documentation is sequencing: connecting every app upfront, rather than 4-6 "mission critical" apps first, is explicitly named as something that "will significantly delay" the point users can start training. Separately, the Notion connector carries a real, structurally-caused oversharing risk — Notion's API doesn't expose document-level permissions to Glean at index time, so any indexed content becomes visible to everyone by default, with query-time Live Mode as the only documented mitigation. On the business-failure side, independent (non-Glean) analysis from Sacra.com attributes real customer churn to "experimental AI budgets" losing out to ROI scrutiny and to complex 4-5 month deployments — but this pass could not find a verbatim CEO quote or a named customer case backing this characterization; it should be treated as a third-party analyst's framing, not a confirmed direct statement.

## Independent read

- Claims 1-2 together are the field's cleanest, most actionable finding: Glean's own documentation is unusually direct about naming its own biggest self-inflicted failure modes (approval delays, over-eager upfront connector rollout) rather than only describing the ideal path.
- Claim 4 is worth flagging carefully — it's a genuine gap between what a specific cited page actually says versus what may have been reasonably inferred or sourced from elsewhere in earlier research. This doesn't mean the downstream-consequences claim is false, only that this specific page isn't where it's documented, and a summary should not cite this URL for that specific consequence claim without further checking.
- Claims 6-7 are this field's most consequential technical finding: the Notion oversharing gap is structural (an API limitation, not a Glean oversight), and the one real fix (Live Mode) shifts the burden onto individual users completing their own OAuth connection — itself a rollout step that can silently fail to happen, which is exactly the kind of "structural workflow friction" this field asks about.
- Claim 8's caveat is important for research integrity: an initial broad web search summarized this as "the CEO acknowledges churn," but a direct fetch of the specific TechCrunch article most likely to contain that quote found no such statement. The claim traces cleanly to Sacra's own analyst commentary, which is credible but should be labeled as analyst framing, not a company admission — a distinction this pass deliberately checked rather than assumed.
- Claim 9's honest "not found" is the right way to handle a real gap — treating the absence of found failure stories as evidence of low failure rates would overstate what a search actually establishes.
