## Headline

Search itself is genuinely designed for non-technical use — plain text, no operators required, natural-language questions work out of the box. But the *entry path* to that experience is gated: a non-technical user's day-one experience depends entirely on whether IT has pre-deployed Glean into Teams/desktop, or left it as a self-install/SSO-tile-only option — the same access-friction pattern already confirmed in this project's Adoption Friction Signals field. Independent G2 reviews corroborate strong day-to-day ease-of-use (4.7/5, 162 reviews, praise for "effortless information retrieval"), but also surface a real caveat: advanced features and reporting customization carry a genuine learning curve. This pass also surfaced a fifth and sixth instance of this project's recurring "similarly-named unrelated company" trap: **GleanQuote** (CPQ software) and **Glean.ai** (already known from earlier fields) both surface in generic "Glean review" searches.

## Independent read

- Claim 1 is the field's most consequential structural finding, and it lines up directly with this project's Adoption Friction Signals field: the *content* of the non-technical experience is good, but *reaching* that experience depends on an IT deployment decision entirely outside the end user's control. A pilot that only measures "was the chat easy to use once open" will systematically overstate real-world UX if it ignores this gate.
- Claims 5-6 together are the most useful independent (non-Glean) corroboration available for this field — a 4.7/5 G2 score with specific supporting quotes is a genuinely strong signal, but claim 6's learning-curve caveat is equally real and should not be dropped just because the headline number is good.
- Claim 7 matters specifically for this field because "ease of use" and "non-technical user" are exactly the kind of generic search terms that pull in GleanQuote and Glean.ai content — more so than for most other fields in this project, where the company-confusion risk is lower. Anyone doing further review-mining for this field should double- and triple-check the company name on every source.
- Claim 8's honest disclosure matters: the G2 numbers cited here are real (they showed up consistently across multiple independent search results, including a specific reviewer name and quote), but a direct first-party fetch failed, so this should be treated as strong-but-not-highest-tier evidence until a live browser confirms it.
