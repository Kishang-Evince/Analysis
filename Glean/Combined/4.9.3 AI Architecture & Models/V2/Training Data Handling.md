## Headline

Glean's own marketing and product docs are consistent and repeated across multiple independent pages: customer data is not used to train the underlying LLMs, backed by zero-retention agreements with model providers, and every customer runs in a fully isolated single-tenant environment (not a shared multi-tenant pool with logical partitioning). What is *not* found anywhere checked is an explicit opt-in/opt-out toggle or setting for training - the "policy" is a standing contractual guarantee, not a switch a tenant admin flips, and the guarantee is explicitly scoped to the "Glean Universal Model Key" deployment path rather than stated as unconditionally uniform across the Bring-Your-Own-Key (BYOK) path.

## Independent read

- Claim 5 is the sharpest, most checkable finding in this field: every public-facing "we don't train on your data" statement (claims 1-3) is phrased as a flat, unconditional guarantee, but the one page that explains *how* that guarantee is actually implemented ties it specifically to the Glean-brokered Universal Model Key path. A tenant on BYOK should not assume the same wording applies without checking their own model-provider contract - this mirrors the same "check your own DPA" pattern the companion Data Leakage Protections field already found for training language generally.
- Claim 4 (sandbox-level architectural isolation) is stronger evidence than a policy statement alone - it describes an infrastructure mechanism (ephemeral, session-scoped, no cross-tenant filesystem access) rather than just a promise, which is a meaningfully different evidentiary tier from claims 1-3.
- Claim 6 is a genuine, honestly-reported absence, not a guess: this field's SOW definition specifically asks about "opt-in/opt-out policies," and no such toggle or setting was located in any documentation surface checked (marketing pages, admin docs, or the model-exclusion feature, which is cost-driven, not training-driven). This should be confirmed or refuted directly with Glean's account team rather than assumed either way.
