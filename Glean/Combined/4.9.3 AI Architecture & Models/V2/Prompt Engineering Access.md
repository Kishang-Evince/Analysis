## Headline

Admins get a real but **narrow and scoped** prompt-configuration surface - a capped set of tenant-wide instructions that only affect response style, not retrieval - plus per-agent-step model/creativity controls at the developer/builder layer. There is no literal model-weight access for anyone (that concept doesn't apply to hosted commercial LLMs anyway); "weights" in this field is best answered as the temperature-style presets and per-step model choice, not raw numeric parameters. The safety-override side is real and configurable, but gated behind a specific paid tier, and the vendor's own published accuracy figures need careful citation - two different, similarly-worded numbers exist for two different things.

## Independent read

- Claim 2 is the most important nuance for a healthcare/compliance buyer: a "content restriction" custom instruction is a **presentation-layer** control, not a **data-access** control. Relying on it to actually prevent sensitive data from being surfaced (rather than just discussed) would be a real misunderstanding of the mechanism - access control for that job lives elsewhere (agent access policies, source-system RBAC), not here.
- Claim 5 is a real licensing gotcha - an evaluator citing prompt-injection defense as a standard Glean capability needs to confirm the tenant/contract actually includes Protect**+**, not base Protect.
- Claim 7 is exactly the kind of imprecision this whole V2 research effort exists to catch - two real, correctly-sourced, differently-scoped numbers that would be easy to sloppily merge into one wrong "accuracy improved to 97.8%" claim.
