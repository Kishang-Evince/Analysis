## Headline

This field has **two genuinely different, opposite-sounding correct answers depending on which model is being asked about** — and conflating them would be inaccurate. The **generative model that produces answers/text** is explicitly never fine-tuned on customer data (confirmed, negative). But the **embedding/ranking model that powers search relevance** is a different story entirely: Glean fine-tunes a distinct, customer-specific version of it continuously on that customer's own documents and usage behavior. Glean's own marketing blurs this distinction by loosely calling the embedding model "their own customized large language model," which invites exactly the kind of confusion this field needs to cut through cleanly.

## Independent read

- For a healthcare/clinical-document buyer asking "will you fine-tune on our clinical documents," the honest, complete answer is: **not the model that generates answers/summaries — that stays untouched by your data. But yes, the search-relevance/embedding layer continuously adapts to your organization's terminology, including document content, as a standard, non-optional part of the product.**
- This matters for a data-handling/compliance review: "fine-tuning" language in a contract or security questionnaire needs to specify *which* model, because Glean's own answer genuinely differs by model type — a blanket "No fine-tuning on customer data" (common shorthand in vendor security reviews) would be technically inaccurate for the embedding layer.
- Claim 5's pipeline detail (MLM, synthetic Q&A, click/co-access signals) is useful context but should be flagged as less rigorously sourced than the directly-quoted first-party claims (3-4, 1-2) — it fills in *how*, not *whether*.
