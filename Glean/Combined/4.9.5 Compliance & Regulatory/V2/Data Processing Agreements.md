## Headline

The DPA is a genuine, comprehensive, multi-jurisdiction document - not a thin GDPR-only template. It explicitly defines "Data Protection Laws" to include GDPR, UK GDPR, Swiss law, **and** CCPA specifically, backs each with real operative clauses (a CCPA-specific no-sale certification, EU SCCs, a separate UK Addendum), and gives subprocessor governance real teeth (30-day advance notice, genuine objection right, termination remedy) rather than notice-only language.

## Independent read

- Claims 2-3 are the field's most precise finding: the DPA doesn't just gesture at "privacy compliance" generically - it names CCPA specifically and includes operative language addressing CCPA's own specific legal tests (the "sale" definition, data-combination restrictions) rather than only GDPR concepts translated loosely.
- Claim 4 is worth emphasizing because subprocessor clauses in many vendor DPAs are notice-only with no real remedy - Glean's includes an actual objection-to-termination pathway, which is a meaningfully stronger customer protection.
- Claim 9's anti-backdoor certification is an unusually specific, strong commitment - worth citing directly in any security-focused summary rather than paraphrasing as generic "law enforcement compliance."
- Claim 10's honest gap (is the public subprocessor page formally Appendix 2?) is a small but real thing worth nailing down directly rather than assuming - the two are very likely the same in practice, but "very likely" isn't "confirmed."
