## Headline

This field is primarily analytical, and this project's own prior research already contains the evidence needed to answer it decisively: Glean started as an enterprise search point solution and has unambiguously become a multi-surface platform - Search, Assistant, Agent Builder, AI Gateway, Model Hub, MCP/A2A interoperability, Memory, Projects, and an admin-facing analytics/help layer (Insights chat/Admin chat) are all separately documented, separately named product surfaces this project has independently confirmed exist and interoperate. The trajectory is platform expansion, not point-solution depth. The long-term-fit implication cuts both ways: broader surface area means more of a client's AI workflow can consolidate onto one vendor (reducing point-tool sprawl), but it also means more surface area to govern, more per-feature model/plan dependencies to track (per this project's own Foundation Models Used findings), and a materially larger blast radius if the platform relationship goes wrong.

## Independent read

- This field did not require new primary research so much as synthesis - the platform-expansion conclusion is already fully supported by evidence this project independently gathered for unrelated reasons across four other sections, which is itself a form of corroboration (the same finding kept surfacing from different angles without anyone setting out to prove it).
- The most decisive single piece of evidence is claim 5: Collections being explicitly marked "Deprecated" in Glean's own documentation in favor of the structurally broader Projects primitive is not a vendor claiming platform ambitions - it's Glean actively retiring a narrower thing in favor of a broader one, which is platform-trajectory behavior, not marketing language.
- Claim 7 is the part of this analysis most worth foregrounding for a healthcare buyer specifically: platform breadth is a genuine value proposition (fewer point tools to manage overall) but it is not free - each new surface (Agent Builder, Memory, Projects, Agent Identity's 19+ integrations) is a new thing Perimeter's own IT/security team would need to understand, govern, and audit, not just adopt.
