## Headline

Glean publishes official SDKs in four languages (Python, TypeScript, Java, Go) plus a Web SDK and a dedicated Indexing SDK, all backed by real, granular rate-limit numbers and a step-by-step custom-datasource guide - a genuinely usable starting point for building a custom integration. But two things a buyer should weigh before committing engineering time: every general-purpose SDK is auto-generated (Speakeasy) and explicitly marked **beta**, with breaking changes possible without a major version bump; and developer/API support issues fall under the **same** Customer Support SLA as general product issues, bucketed as "Normal" priority (1 business week initial response on Standard tier) rather than a dedicated, faster developer-support track.

## Independent read

- Claims 2 and 6 are the field's most decision-relevant findings, and both cut against a naive "Glean has great SDKs and support" assumption: the SDKs are explicitly beta/auto-generated (not hand-polished, first-party-maintained clients), and a broken custom API integration is contractually a "Normal," not expedited, priority under the standard support tier - a real staffing/risk input for whoever owns this integration in production.
- Claim 4 is a genuine strength: the rate limits are specific enough (down to `/indexdocuments` at 600 docs/minute, `/processalldocuments` at once per 3 hours) that an integration engineer can actually design a bulk-load job around real numbers, not guesswork.
- Claim 7 (the developer-docs MCP server) is a distinctive, positive differentiator worth surfacing on its own - it's not something this project's earlier API-architecture research (which covered Glean's *product* MCP server for search/chat/agents) had captured, since this is a separate MCP server specifically for the documentation itself.
