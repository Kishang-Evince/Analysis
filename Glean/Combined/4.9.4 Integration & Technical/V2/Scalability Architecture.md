## Headline

Glean does not publish hard ceilings on users, documents, or query volume - no page found states a maximum tenant size. What *is* published is a concrete, numeric API rate-limit schedule (per-endpoint requests/second, a deployment-wide cap, and a per-user-token cap), plus real-world scale signals: Glean's own growth numbers (27B+ documents indexed platform-wide, 20 trillion tokens/year processed) and named customer deployments up to 14,000 employees. A third-party infrastructure-cost analysis also raises a concrete, checkable concern about compute scaling for the Customer-Hosted (BYO-cloud) deployment path specifically.

## Independent read

- Claim 1 is the field's honest headline: Glean, like most enterprise SaaS vendors, does not publish hard tenant-size ceilings. That absence is itself the correct, evaluable finding for this field - not a research failure.
- Claims 2-4 are the strongest concrete data this field can offer: a real, numbered API rate-limit schedule is a genuine, checkable transaction-volume constraint, and the sharp gap between generative-endpoint limits (0.5 req/s) and search-endpoint limits (5-10 req/s) is directly actionable for capacity planning around any agent-automation use case.
- Claim 7 needs a clear caveat: gosearch.ai is a competing enterprise-search vendor, so its infrastructure-cost teardown of Glean should be read as a competitive argument, not a neutral benchmark - it is included because it is the only concrete compute-sizing data point found for the Customer-Hosted path, not because it is independently verified. It is flagged as **unverified/third-party** in the Confidence section below, distinct from the Doc-Verified claims.
- Claim 6 (named customer scale) is useful only as an existence proof, not a limit - a buyer should not read "14,000 employees at Booking.com" as "Glean's maximum," only as "Glean has operated successfully at this scale."

**Doc-Verified** for claims 1, 2, 3, 4, 8 (fetched directly from `developers.glean.com` and `docs.glean.com`). **Doc-Verified (first-party, vendor-published)** for claim 5 (Glean's own blog). **Search-corroborated, not independently re-fetched from the primary press source** for claim 6 (customer employee counts). **Unverified/third-party competitive source** for claim 7 (gosearch.ai) - explicitly flagged, not treated as equivalent-confidence to the first-party claims. **Absence-check** for the core question this field asks (documented hard limits on users/data/transaction volume) - none found, stated explicitly rather than inferred. 4 independent source domains, validation date 2026-09-14.
