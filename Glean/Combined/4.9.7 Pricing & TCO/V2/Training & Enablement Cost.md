## Headline

No source checked this pass - Glean's own marketing, its own website-terms contract language, or third-party pricing aggregators - names a specific dollar cost for training or certification. Glean Academy (covered separately by this project's `4.9.6 Adoption & Readiness/V2/Training Infrastructure.md`) is public, free-to-attend guest-lecture content with no price gate found anywhere. More significantly, a direct read of Glean's own website-terms contract (Exhibit A, Section 3 - the same primary legal source that resolved the Premium Support fee question for the Ongoing Maintenance Cost field) is entirely silent on training and enablement as a line item: it names free native-connector setup, paid custom-connector professional services, a dedicated CSM/Solutions Architect, and a 12%-of-license premium support option - but never mentions training, enablement, or certification at all. Combined with the sibling Training Infrastructure field's confirmed finding that no customer-facing certification program exists in any form, the honest conclusion is that customer training cost is an undocumented gap, not a confirmed "included" or "separately licensed" answer - closing it requires a direct question to the Glean account team, which is what the companion test guide is built to do.

## Independent read

- The headline finding here is a genuine, confirmed absence rather than a discovered price: this project now has strong primary-source evidence (the Exhibit A contract read directly, twice, across two sibling fields) that training/enablement cost is simply not addressed in Glean's standard commercial terms - unlike Premium Support, which the same document prices explicitly at 12% of license fee. That asymmetry is itself the most useful, citable fact for this field.
- Claim 3's hiring-signal finding is worth including specifically because it prevents this field from reading as a flat "no" on certification - the honest picture is "not today, but Glean appears to be staffing toward building one," which is a materially different planning input for a client scoping a multi-year deployment.
- Claim 4 is included mainly as a boundary-drawing exercise: it would be easy to see "2,000+ accreditations" in a search result and mistakenly cite it as evidence of a robust, cost-bearing customer certification program, when it actually describes a separate partner-ecosystem training track.
- Claim 7 is flagged plainly as an inference, not a finding, in keeping with this project's convention of not letting analogical reasoning masquerade as a doc-verified fact.
