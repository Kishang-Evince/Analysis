## Headline

Both commercial models are confirmed as genuinely hybrid: a per-seat monthly fee plus a pooled, metered consumption layer (FlexCredits for Enterprise Flex, Model Hub token pass-through for Core Suite). This pass surfaced real, previously-undocumented dollar figures on the Core Suite side - Glean's own Model Hub page publishes actual per-million-token rates (Glean's own Waldo model at $0.50 input/$2.50 output; GPT-4o at $2.50/$10.00; Claude Opus 5 at $5.00/$25.00) - a genuine, citable pricing data point Glean does publish, contradicting the general "Glean publishes no list prices" framing for this one specific line item. Separately, this pass found a real discrepancy worth flagging: the usage-limits page does not mention a ">30 FlexCredits counts as multiple queries" rule anywhere, and alert thresholds are 50/75/90/100%, not 80% - these specific base-doc details could not be re-confirmed and should be checked directly against the live product before being relied on.

## Independent read

- Claim 5 is this pass's most valuable new finding: it directly nuances the "Glean publishes no prices" framing carried over from the base research - that framing is true for seat prices and FlexCredit-pack dollar costs, but false for Model Hub per-token rates, which are genuinely public and specific. A summary that says "Glean publishes no pricing anywhere" would now be inaccurate.
- Claim 8's discrepancy is worth taking seriously rather than smoothing over: a specific numeric rule (">30 FlexCredits") and a specific alert-threshold example (80%) from earlier research could not be re-confirmed on the exact page that should document them. This doesn't necessarily mean the earlier claim was wrong - thresholds may be tenant-configurable and an 80% example may have simply been illustrative rather than a fixed platform default - but it should be re-checked directly rather than repeated with confidence.
- Claim 9 is included specifically to protect this field's credibility: the internet has no shortage of specific-sounding Glean pricing numbers, and a large share of the most detailed ones trace back to parties with a reason to make Glean look costly. This is worth stating plainly rather than laundering competitor marketing copy into a "confirmed" pricing figure.
