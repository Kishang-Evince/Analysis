## Headline

The base research's core structural conclusion still holds after fresh checks: **no complete, itemized 3-year TCO figure exists anywhere, Glean-authored or third-party.** What this pass adds is real precision to several individual line items feeding that model — a real published Model Hub rate card, a real (and now-expired) FlexCredit grace period confirmed directly from a legal document, and a materially more credible third-party annual-spend benchmark (Vendr.com's real-transaction median) than the marketing-blog figures found elsewhere. A rough, heavily-caveated order-of-magnitude range can now be triangulated for Year 1 alone — but it is built by stacking multiple partially-confirmed and unconfirmed figures together, and should never be presented as a quote or a confident estimate.

## Independent read

- Claim 1 is worth restating plainly every time this field is discussed: a 141% ROI figure with no visible cost breakdown is not evidence about cost magnitude in either direction — it says nothing about whether the underlying spend is small or large, only that Forrester's model judged the benefits to exceed whatever that spend was.
- Claim 2's honest "we don't know which number is right" is the correct way to handle a real, found discrepancy rather than silently picking the more convenient 100-seat figure — this should be the first question asked in an actual sales conversation.
- Claims 3-5 are the genuine value-add of this pass: this field's job is synthesis, and this pass had access to real primary-source findings (a legal PDF's dated grace period, real published token rates, real transaction-data benchmarks) that the earlier base research did not have. Folding those in with clear attribution is more useful than re-deriving the same "cannot compute TCO" conclusion without the added precision.
- Claim 6 is offered carefully, with the explicit warning built into the claim itself, because a specific-looking dollar range is exactly the kind of output that risks being copied into a client deliverable without its uncertainty intact. The range exists to show what triangulation from currently-available information looks like — not to replace the real quote this field's own verification steps call for.
