# Which team should lead your AI search implementation project

[Enterprise AI search](https://www.glean.com/blog/enterprise-ai-search-rag) implementation demands more than technical expertise — it requires leaders who can orchestrate complex organizational transformation while managing sophisticated technology deployments. The most successful implementations emerge when leadership understands that AI search fundamentally changes how employees find information, make decisions, and collaborate across departments.

Organizations implementing AI search face a unique challenge: while the technology promises to revolutionize knowledge management and workflow automation, 85% of AI projects fail to deliver on their business promises. In fact, [MIT research](https://fortune.com/2025/08/18/mit-report-95-percent-generative-ai-pilots-at-companies-failing-cfo/) found that 95% of generative AI pilots at companies are failing to achieve rapid revenue acceleration or measurable ROI six months post-pilot, with the biggest ROI coming from back-office automation even as over half of GenAI budgets go to sales and marketing tools. This failure rate stems not from technical shortcomings but from leadership approaches that treat AI search as purely an IT initiative rather than an enterprise-wide transformation.

This failure rate stems not from technical shortcomings but from leadership approaches that treat AI search as purely an IT initiative rather than an enterprise-wide transformation, with approximately 70% of AI implementation failures attributable to organizational and human factors.

The shift from traditional enterprise search to AI-powered systems introduces complexities that extend far beyond algorithms and infrastructure. Leaders must navigate ethical considerations around data usage, manage the psychological impact of human-AI collaboration, and guide cultural changes that touch every corner of the organization — from engineering teams seeking technical documentation to HR departments streamlining employee onboarding.

## What defines AI search implementation leadership

AI search implementation leadership transcends traditional technology management roles, demanding a rare combination of technical acumen, strategic vision, and deep understanding of organizational psychology. Unlike conventional IT projects that focus primarily on system deployment and maintenance, AI search initiatives require leaders who can simultaneously manage technical infrastructure, guide cultural transformation, and navigate the complex ethical landscapes that emerge when AI systems begin making decisions that previously required human judgment.

The limitations become apparent when examining failed AI implementations. Companies like Zillow lost over $300 million when their AI initiatives failed to account for organizational readiness and market dynamics; Zillow's AI-driven home valuation system overestimated property values during aggressive scaling, leading to inventory [write-downs](https://developmentcorporate.com/uncategorized/zillow-ai-goes-crazy-causes-8-billion-drop-in-market-cap-a-304-million-operating-loss-and-2000-jobs/) and layoffs affecting 25% of the workforce. California State University faced fierce opposition to their AI implementation plan despite having clear technical specifications, because leadership failed to address human concerns about the technology's impact on jobs and academic integrity. These failures highlight a crucial gap: technical competence alone cannot ensure AI search success.

### The evolution from IT management to transformation leadership

Traditional IT leadership structures, designed for an era of predictable software deployments and system maintenance, prove inadequate for AI search implementation. While CIOs and IT directors excel at managing technical operations, research shows that 61% of technology leaders now spend more time on operational functions than strategic responsibilities — precisely when AI initiatives demand the opposite focus. This operational burden leaves little bandwidth for addressing the broader implications of AI search: how it changes decision-making processes, impacts employee workflows, and requires new forms of cross-functional collaboration.

### Core competencies of effective AI search leaders

Successful AI search implementation requires leaders who possess a unique blend of capabilities that span technical, organizational, and ethical domains:

-   **Technical fluency with strategic perspective**: Leaders must understand concepts like [retrieval-augmented generation (RAG)](https://www.glean.com/blog/retrieval-augmented-generation-rag-the-key-to-enabling-generative-ai-for-the-enterprise), [vector databases](https://www.glean.com/blog/unlocking-the-power-of-vector-search-in-enterprise), and [large language models](https://www.glean.com/blog/enterprise-search-llm-tech) — not to build these systems themselves, but to make informed decisions about architecture, vendor selection, and resource allocation. They need sufficient technical knowledge to evaluate whether a simple API integration suffices or if custom model development is necessary.
    
-   **Organizational change expertise**: Beyond technology, these leaders must guide transformation across departments, each with distinct needs and concerns. Sales teams require different AI search capabilities than engineering departments; HR's compliance requirements differ from marketing's creative needs. Leaders must orchestrate these varied requirements into a cohesive implementation strategy.
    
-   **Ethical navigation and governance**: AI search systems access vast amounts of organizational data, raising questions about privacy, bias, and appropriate use. Leaders must establish clear protocols for data access, implement safeguards against algorithmic bias, and create governance structures that ensure AI search enhances rather than compromises organizational values.
    
-   **Human-AI collaboration design**: Perhaps most critically, AI search leaders must understand how to design systems that augment human capabilities rather than replace them. This involves creating interfaces that feel intuitive, establishing clear boundaries for AI decision-making authority, and ensuring employees understand when and how to leverage AI search effectively.
    

The most effective AI search leaders view their role as bridging multiple worlds: connecting technical possibilities with business realities, aligning diverse stakeholder interests, and translating between the language of data scientists and the needs of end users. They recognize that successful AI search implementation isn't measured solely by query response times or accuracy rates, but by how effectively the technology enables employees to access knowledge, make better decisions, and focus on work that truly requires human creativity and judgment.

## Why traditional IT teams aren't enough

Traditional IT teams excel at maintaining technological systems but often lack the strategic foresight necessary for AI search implementations. While they are crucial for managing infrastructure, these teams typically address immediate technical needs over transformative initiatives. This focus can impede AI adoption, which demands a holistic approach involving both technology and organizational change.

A significant challenge lies in aligning leadership priorities. Although many IT leaders are recognized as innovators, a smaller percentage actively engage in transformative projects. This misalignment reflects a broader issue: as enterprises aim to become data-driven, cultural barriers frequently stand in the way. Overcoming these obstacles requires leadership that can effectively integrate technology with organizational goals.

Moreover, the responsibilities of CIOs often skew towards operational tasks, which can limit their capacity to foster innovation. This tendency restricts their ability to address the strategic aspects of AI search, such as promoting cross-departmental collaboration and ensuring responsible data use. As a result, teams may maintain systems efficiently but struggle with managing the broader transformation that successful AI projects demand.

Without a [comprehensive strategy](https://www.glean.com/blog/create-ai-strategy-2024) that aligns technical efforts with business objectives, AI search implementations can fall short. To truly capitalize on AI's potential, organizations need leaders who can bridge technical solutions with strategic goals and cultivate a culture of continuous improvement.

## The rise of cross-functional AI leadership

In today's rapidly evolving landscape, AI projects demand leaders who can seamlessly integrate strategic initiatives across the organization. This requires a new breed of leadership that transcends traditional boundaries, focusing on holistic transformation rather than isolated technical execution.

### Chief innovation and transformation officers

These officers play a crucial role in steering AI projects towards success by fostering an environment that encourages innovation and embraces change. Their expertise in aligning diverse teams under a unified vision sets the stage for effective implementation.

-   **Holistic integration**: They ensure that AI initiatives align with organizational goals, breaking down silos and promoting synergy across departments.
-   **Driving change**: By championing adaptive work cultures, they facilitate the transition to AI-enhanced processes, ensuring teams are prepared to leverage new technologies.
-   **Risk management**: They implement comprehensive frameworks to address potential challenges, ensuring that innovation does not compromise ethical standards.

### Expanded technology leadership roles

As AI becomes central to business strategy, organizations are redefining leadership roles to encompass broader responsibilities. This involves not only expanding existing positions but also creating roles that prioritize strategic impact over operational tasks.

-   **Enterprise-wide focus**: These leaders oversee diverse teams, ensuring AI projects contribute to overarching business objectives and deliver measurable value.
-   **Fostering innovation**: By encouraging collaborative approaches, they cultivate a culture of continuous improvement and agile adaptation.
-   **Strategic alignment**: Prioritizing strategic delivery, they ensure AI efforts enhance organizational efficiency and competitiveness.

This evolved leadership framework is essential for harnessing AI's potential, ensuring it drives substantial and sustainable benefits throughout the enterprise.

## Essential team composition for AI search projects

Crafting an effective AI search project team involves assembling a diverse range of skills that combine technical expertise with strategic insight. This ensures smooth implementation and robust system performance, allowing organizations to navigate AI complexities confidently.

### Core technical roles

Data scientists are crucial for transforming raw data into actionable insights. They build machine learning models and handle data preparation, ensuring accurate predictions. Working alongside them, machine learning engineers focus on deploying these models, managing pipelines, and monitoring system performance to maintain efficiency.

Data engineers are responsible for creating and upholding the data infrastructure, focusing on integrity and security. AI engineers design the architecture and develop APIs, integrating AI capabilities into existing systems to enhance functionality.

AI governance strategists develop risk management frameworks and ensure compliance with privacy regulations. Their work is vital for maintaining organizational trust and upholding ethical standards. Recent findings show that [77%](https://iapp.org/resources/article/ai-governance-profession-report/) of organizations are currently working on AI governance programs, jumping to nearly 90% for organizations already using AI. Only 1.5% of surveyed organizations report they won't need additional staff for AI governance in the next 12 months.

### Strategic and governance roles

AI project managers ensure technical initiatives align with organizational objectives. They oversee project timelines and coordinate between teams, driving projects towards strategic goals.

AI ethicists focus on maintaining the integrity of AI systems by addressing potential biases and setting ethical standards. Domain experts bring industry-specific insights, validating AI outputs and ensuring practical applications meet business needs.

AI governance strategists develop risk management frameworks and ensure compliance with privacy regulations. Their work is vital for maintaining organizational trust and upholding ethical standards.

### Operational support roles

LLM operations engineers ensure model efficiency and manage scaling, maintaining robust infrastructure. AI engagement managers facilitate communication between stakeholders and support the adoption of AI technologies, aiding in organizational transitions.

Data analysts interpret the impact of AI on business metrics, providing valuable insights that guide strategic decisions. Their analysis helps organizations understand the contributions of AI projects to overall business performance.

By assembling a comprehensive team that marries technical skills with strategic foresight, organizations can ensure their AI search projects are both successful and transformative.

## Building your AI search implementation team

### Start with leadership alignment

A successful AI search implementation begins with cohesive leadership. Clearly delineate roles among IT, business, and transformation leaders to ensure aligned objectives and smooth project execution. This alignment creates a unified vision that integrates AI initiatives with the organization's strategic direction.

Develop comprehensive guidelines for AI decision-making processes. These guidelines should specify the extent of AI authority and incorporate necessary human oversight. By establishing these parameters, organizations can maintain a balance that respects both automated processes and human input, ensuring ethical and effective AI system operation.

### Scale team based on implementation approach

Tailoring your team to the project's complexity enhances efficiency. Simple API integration projects require streamlined teams with focused technical skills. Conversely, custom solutions necessitate a full spectrum of technical expertise to address unique business demands and ensure tailored implementation.

For RAG implementations, engage specialists in data retrieval to leverage nuanced capabilities. A hybrid team model that combines internal and external resources can provide the flexibility and specialized skills needed for diverse project requirements, optimizing resource allocation and expertise.

### Focus on collaboration enablement

Fostering collaboration is vital for the success of AI search projects. Deploy tools that enhance knowledge sharing across departments, ensuring that information flows freely and supports cohesive decision-making. Establishing clear communication pathways between technical and business teams encourages alignment and leverages diverse insights.

Regular cross-functional meetings and progress assessments help maintain project momentum. These practices create a dynamic feedback environment where user insights inform development, driving continuous improvement and aligning the project with evolving business needs.

The right leadership structure and team composition will determine whether your AI search implementation becomes a transformative force or another failed technology initiative. Success requires more than technical expertise — it demands leaders who can navigate organizational change, foster cross-functional collaboration, and balance innovation with responsible governance.
