## Understanding AI agents

AI agents represent a fundamental shift in how organizations approach workflow automation and knowledge management. Unlike traditional software that requires constant human direction, AI agents operate as autonomous systems that perceive their environment, make decisions, and take actions to achieve specific goals. These systems combine advanced language models with sophisticated reasoning frameworks, memory capabilities, and tool integration to function as digital team members rather than mere automation tools.

The evolution from basic AI models to fully autonomous agents marks a significant technological leap. While early AI implementations focused on single-task completion - answering questions or generating content - modern agents employ the ReAct framework (Reasoning + Acting) to break down complex requests into manageable steps. This architectural approach enables agents to:

-   **Plan strategically**: Agents analyze tasks to create multi-step execution plans, considering available resources and potential obstacles
-   **Execute autonomously**: They perform actions across multiple systems without requiring step-by-step human guidance
-   **Learn and adapt**: Through memory systems and feedback loops, agents improve their performance over time based on past interactions
-   **Collaborate effectively**: Advanced agents work alongside human teams and other AI systems to accomplish shared objectives

### The technology behind enterprise AI agents

Enterprise-grade AI agents require a robust technology stack comprising three essential layers. The foundation layer includes the core [language models](https://www.glean.com/blog/enterprise-language-models-choosing-the-right-approach-for-your-business-needs) and input processing capabilities. Models like GPT-4o provide natural language understanding and multimodal reasoning, while specialized systems handle speech-to-text conversion and image processing. This foundational infrastructure determines the agent's ability to understand and interact with various data formats across the enterprise.

The reasoning and planning layer transforms these foundation models into intelligent actors. Frameworks such as ReAct and function calling mechanisms enable agents to decompose complex tasks, maintain context across extended interactions, and make informed decisions. Memory systems - both short-term for immediate task context and long-term for organizational knowledge - allow agents to build upon previous experiences and maintain consistency across interactions.

Engineering teams leverage AI agents to accelerate development cycles through intelligent code review, automated debugging, and proactive identification of potential issues. Rather than replacing developers, these agents function as force multipliers - handling routine tasks while engineers focus on complex problem-solving and innovation. Sales organizations deploy agents that analyze customer interactions, identify opportunities, and prepare personalized proposals by synthesizing data from CRM systems, market intelligence platforms, and internal knowledge repositories. Access to AI assistance increases customer support worker productivity by [15%](https://academic.oup.com/qje/article/140/2/889/7990658) on average, with less skilled workers seeing a 30% increase in issues resolved per hour.

The transformation from theoretical capability to practical application reveals the true value of AI agents in enterprise settings. These agents don't simply provide scripted responses; they understand context, identify root causes, and take appropriate action while maintaining compliance with company policies.

The key differentiator between AI agents and traditional automation lies in their ability to handle exceptions and adapt to new situations. When an automated workflow encounters an unexpected scenario, it typically fails or requires human intervention. AI agents, however, can reason through novel situations, seek additional information when needed, and adjust their approach based on the specific context - much like a skilled human worker would.

## Tips on AI agent evaluation

Evaluating AI agents strategically positions your organization to harness their full potential. The evaluation process should consider several critical aspects to ensure the agent aligns with your enterprise's objectives and delivers measurable outcomes.

### 1\. Prioritize integration

Seamless integration ensures that AI agents work harmoniously within your existing infrastructure. Evaluate how well an agent can interface with your current software and data systems. The goal is to facilitate uninterrupted data exchange and cohesive operations across platforms.

-   **Enhance system interactions**: Ensure that agents can easily connect with existing applications, allowing for efficient workflows and effective data utilization. This involves checking compatibility with existing APIs and adherence to your organization's data management protocols.

### 2\. Focus on scalability

Scalability ensures that the AI agent can grow alongside your business, accommodating increased demands without losing efficiency. Consider whether the agent's framework supports expansion in line with your organization's evolving needs.

-   **Support for business growth**: Select agents that are designed to handle higher data volumes and more complex tasks as your enterprise expands. This involves assessing how the agent can maintain performance across various operational scales and adapt to new business requirements.
