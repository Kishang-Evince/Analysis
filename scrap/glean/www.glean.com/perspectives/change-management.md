# Types of change management models explained

Organizations across every industry face an accelerating pace of transformation driven by technological innovation, market disruption, and evolving customer expectations. The ability to navigate these shifts successfully has become a critical differentiator between companies that thrive and those that struggle to survive.

Change management has emerged as the essential discipline for bridging this gap. It provides structured approaches and proven methodologies that help organizations move from their current state to their desired future while maintaining productivity and employee engagement throughout the transition. In fact, [31%](https://www.oak.com/blog/10-shocking-workplace-change-statistics-for-2023/) of chief executive officers lose their positions due to insufficient performance in managing organizational change, while 73% of employees affected by change experience moderate to high stress levels. Additionally, 25% of organizations fail to review past change management initiatives, creating patterns where the same mistakes repeat across subsequent transformation efforts.

Yet research consistently shows that 50 to 70 percent of organizational change initiatives fail to achieve their intended outcomes. In fact, recent [Bain research](https://www.bain.com/about/media-center/press-releases/2024/88-of-business-transformations-fail-to-achieve-their-original-ambitions-those-that-succeed-avoid-overloading-top-talent/) of more than 400 executives finds that 88% of business transformations fail to achieve their original ambitions—only 12% reach their intended goals—often because organizations overload their top talent, leading to burnout among those most critical to success. This stark reality underscores the gap between recognizing the need for change and actually implementing it effectively across complex enterprise environments.

Change management has emerged as the essential discipline for bridging this gap. It provides structured approaches and proven methodologies that help organizations move from their current state to their desired future while maintaining productivity and employee engagement throughout the transition.

## What is change management?

Change management is a systematic discipline that focuses on preparing, equipping, and supporting individuals as they adopt new ways of working within an organization. Unlike [project management](https://www.glean.com/blog/ai-prompts-for-project-managers), which concentrates on the technical aspects of implementing new systems or processes, change management addresses the human dimensions of transformation — the behaviors, mindsets, and cultural shifts required for sustainable success.

The discipline encompasses both structured methodologies and flexible strategies designed to minimize resistance while maximizing adoption across all organizational levels. Effective change management integrates planning, communication, training, and reinforcement activities that help employees understand not just what is changing, but why it matters and how they fit into the future state. This comprehensive approach ensures that investments in new technologies, processes, or organizational structures deliver their intended value.

At its core, change management recognizes a fundamental truth: organizations don't change — people do. Technical implementations may proceed flawlessly, but without addressing the human elements of transition, even the most sophisticated systems will fail to deliver results. This reality has driven the evolution of change management from a peripheral consideration to a critical success factor in organizational transformation.

The scope of change management extends beyond single projects or initiatives. It encompasses everything from large-scale [digital transformations](https://www.glean.com/blog/top-3-it-trends-impacting-business-success-in-2023) and mergers to departmental restructuring and process improvements. Regardless of scale, successful change management follows similar principles: clear vision, strong leadership, stakeholder engagement, and systematic support for individuals navigating the transition. These elements work together to create an environment where change becomes not just possible, but sustainable.

Modern change management also recognizes that organizational transformation rarely follows a linear path. The discipline has evolved to accommodate the complexity and interconnectedness of today's business environment, where changes in one area ripple throughout the entire organization. This systems-thinking approach helps leaders anticipate challenges, identify dependencies, and coordinate efforts across multiple teams and departments to achieve cohesive transformation.

## Why organizations need change management

Implementing change management methodologies enhances project outcomes significantly. Studies show that structured change efforts can drastically improve success rates. In fact, companies with robust change management practices average [143%](https://milestonetask.com/change-management-success-rates-and-roi/) return on investment from their transformation initiatives, compared to just 35% for organizations without structured change management—a four-fold difference that translates into substantial financial impact for enterprise-wide initiatives. By aligning resources and strategies, organizations can prevent delays, budget excesses, and poor adoption—ensuring that every initiative meets its intended objectives.

## Key components of change management models

Successful change management relies on several critical components that ensure the seamless transition of an organization from its current state to a desired future. These components form the backbone of any effective change management strategy, providing a structured yet adaptable framework to accommodate the unique needs of each organization.

### Leadership and sponsorship

Engaging stakeholders early in the process builds crucial buy-in and minimizes resistance. Involving affected groups in planning and decision-making helps address concerns and integrates valuable insights. Organizations where senior managers communicate openly about transformation progress are [8 times](https://www.mckinsey.com/capabilities/people-and-organizational-performance/our-insights/successful-transformations) more likely to experience successful change, with enterprise-wide transformations being 12.4 times more likely to succeed when senior managers communicate continually. Despite this, nearly half of survey respondents indicated they would spend more time communicating if their transformation happened again. This collaborative approach fosters ownership and accountability, essential for successful implementation.

### Communication strategies

Crafting clear, consistent messages is vital to align stakeholders with the change vision. Communication should explain the strategic importance of the transformation and its implications for all involved. By tailoring messages to different audiences, organizations ensure everyone comprehends the change’s significance and their part in it.

### Stakeholder engagement

Engaging stakeholders early in the process builds crucial buy-in and minimizes resistance. Involving affected groups in planning and decision-making helps address concerns and integrates valuable insights. This collaborative approach fosters ownership and accountability, essential for successful implementation.

### Training and capability building

Providing targeted training programs equips employees with the necessary skills and knowledge for new roles. Such programs should be customized to meet the specific demands of the transformation. This investment empowers employees to contribute effectively in the new organizational landscape.

### Resistance management

Proactively addressing resistance is crucial to facilitating adoption. Identifying the root causes of concerns allows for tailored interventions. By tackling fears head-on, organizations can transform potential obstacles into opportunities for growth and stability.

### Culture alignment

Aligning changes with organizational values and behaviors ensures sustained success. Transformations should complement, rather than disrupt, existing cultural strengths. Ensuring strategic coherence with core values supports seamless integration into the organizational fabric.

### Measurement and reinforcement

Monitoring progress and celebrating milestones help maintain momentum. By establishing clear metrics, organizations can evaluate the effectiveness of change initiatives. Celebrating achievements reinforces positive behaviors, motivating continued effort and embedding changes into the organization.

## Popular change management frameworks

Organizations often rely on established frameworks to guide them through effective transformation. These frameworks offer structured methodologies tailored to unique organizational needs, ensuring smoother transitions and improved outcomes.

### Kotter's 8-step process

Kotter's approach emphasizes the importance of initiating change with urgency. By forming a dedicated coalition of leaders, organizations can harness diverse strengths to drive the initiative forward. Establishing a clear vision and actionable strategies provides a cohesive roadmap for all stakeholders.

Communication plays a critical role—mobilizing a network to disseminate the vision ensures widespread engagement. Removing obstacles empowers teams to act, while achieving short-term wins boosts morale and momentum. Continuous improvement maintains progress, embedding change within the organizational culture.

### ADKAR Model

The ADKAR Model focuses on individual transformation through five stages. Building awareness aligns personal and organizational objectives, fostering a shared understanding of the change. Encouraging desire motivates individuals to actively engage in the process, while knowledge equips them with necessary skills.

Translating knowledge into ability empowers employees to confidently implement new practices. Reinforcement solidifies changes, making them integral to daily operations. This model highlights the significance of personal commitment in successful change management.

### Lewin's three-stage model

Lewin's model presents a straightforward approach to managing change. The initial stage involves preparing the organization by challenging existing norms and creating readiness for new ideas. The change phase introduces new methods and behaviors, demanding adaptability and openness.

The final stage focuses on embedding these changes, ensuring they become part of the organizational fabric. Lewin's framework emphasizes the iterative nature of change, encouraging ongoing reflection and refinement.

### Bridges' transition model

Bridges' model addresses the emotional journey of change. The initial phase involves helping individuals release old habits, acknowledging the emotional challenges involved. The transition phase navigates the ambiguity between old and new states, requiring careful guidance to maintain focus.

Embracing new beginnings encourages the adoption of innovative practices and mindsets. This model underscores the importance of addressing emotional responses, ensuring individuals feel supported throughout the transition process.

## Implementing change management strategies

Success in change management begins with defining the transformation's scope, impacts, and clear success metrics. This clarity ensures alignment and provides a measurable path forward, allowing organizations to focus resources effectively and maintain direction.

### Assessing organizational readiness

Understanding an organization's readiness for change involves evaluating current capabilities and potential challenges. Conducting thorough assessments helps tailor strategies to support areas needing reinforcement, ensuring a smoother transition and minimizing resistance.

### Crafting detailed plans

Developing detailed plans that encompass communication, training, and support is essential for effective execution. These plans should outline information dissemination, training delivery, and necessary support structures, fostering an environment that supports successful change adoption.

### Feedback and monitoring systems

Establishing robust feedback and monitoring systems allows organizations to track progress and make informed adjustments. Regular stakeholder check-ins provide insights into the effectiveness of strategies, enabling continuous refinement and adaptation to changing circumstances.

Building strong networks of change agents throughout the organization enhances implementation efforts. These individuals act as champions and resources, facilitating communication and addressing concerns, ultimately empowering teams to embrace change collaboratively.

### Utilizing data-driven insights

Leveraging data-driven insights to monitor adoption and identify areas needing attention is crucial. Analyzing patterns and trends helps allocate resources efficiently, ensuring that interventions are targeted and effective.

### Aligning short and long-term objectives

Balancing short-term achievements with long-term transformation goals is vital for sustained success. Quick wins demonstrate progress, while long-term objectives ensure lasting impact, keeping the organization focused and motivated throughout the change journey.

Involving individuals who are hesitant about change can provide valuable perspectives that improve the overall strategy. Contrary to common stereotypes, younger employees actually demonstrate the highest resistance to change, with workers aged 16-24 proving most resistant at [41%](https://pollackpeacebuilding.com/blog/change-management-statistics/), followed by 36% of those aged 35-44. Resistance decreases progressively with age, dropping to just 28% among employees aged 55 and older. Engaging them as collaborators rather than opponents allows organizations to anticipate challenges and co-create solutions. This approach not only reduces resistance but also builds a sense of ownership and commitment toward the change.

## Overcoming resistance to change

Understanding the human reaction to uncertainty is crucial for navigating resistance effectively. Organizations should focus on identifying the specific concerns that individuals may have. By fostering an environment of openness and trust, leaders can tailor their strategies to address both logical and emotional aspects of resistance.

Involving individuals who are hesitant about change can provide valuable perspectives that improve the overall strategy. Engaging them as collaborators rather than opponents allows organizations to anticipate challenges and co-create solutions. This approach not only reduces resistance but also builds a sense of ownership and commitment toward the change.

Facilitating [open communication channels](https://www.glean.com/blog/intranet-best-practices) helps employees voice their apprehensions and seek clarity. Highlighting the benefits of change at both personal and team levels can enhance motivation and acceptance. Sharing success stories and leveraging peer support empowers individuals, fostering confidence and a collective commitment to the transformation journey.

Allowing time for adaptation and providing resources for personal development are key. Change is a complex process that requires patience and empathy. By offering tailored guidance and support, organizations can help individuals transition smoothly, cultivating resilience and a readiness for future challenges.

## Measuring change management success

Determining the effectiveness of change management efforts involves setting specific success criteria. Establishing key performance indicators (KPIs) upfront guides the evaluation process, ensuring that change efforts align with the organization’s strategic priorities. This foundation allows for a focused assessment of how well objectives are being met.

### Tracking essential metrics

Organizations should incorporate a balanced approach, measuring both hard data—such as efficiency gains and cost reductions—and qualitative insights like employee satisfaction and adaptability. This dual focus provides a holistic view of the change impact. Regular feedback mechanisms, such as brief surveys, offer timely insights into employee experiences and highlight areas for enhancement.

Understanding how quickly new practices are adopted compared to initial plans can reveal the effectiveness of implementation strategies. Evaluating the retention of skills from training sessions ensures that employees are prepared to support the change. Analyzing the utilization of change management resources provides further insights into their practical application and relevance.

### Embracing improvement and transparency

Reflecting on initiatives after completion allows for the identification of valuable insights and learning opportunities. These reflections contribute to a culture of ongoing refinement and innovation. Creating real-time dashboards enhances transparency, enabling leaders to visualize progress and make informed decisions, thus maintaining the momentum of change efforts.

## Building organizational change capability

To excel in a rapidly changing landscape, organizations must cultivate change management as a foundational strength. This involves nurturing leadership that champions innovation and sees transformation as a strategic asset. By fostering an environment that embraces adaptability, companies can develop a workforce poised for continuous improvement.

Creating a specialized change management unit provides a centralized point for coordinating efforts and resources. This hub ensures a consistent approach across initiatives, supporting organization-wide transformation. Investing in specialized training and professional certification equips teams with the expertise needed to navigate complex changes effectively.

### Harnessing knowledge and innovation

Building a repository of successful strategies and lessons learned creates a valuable resource for future initiatives. This collection of insights enables teams to adapt tried-and-true methods to fit specific contexts. Sharing these insights encourages a culture of innovation and collaboration, driving continuous enhancement.

Recognizing individuals who excel in change leadership reinforces a culture of resilience and adaptability. Such acknowledgment motivates others and establishes benchmarks for success. Incorporating change readiness into strategic plans ensures alignment with organizational objectives, positioning the company for proactive growth.

### Fostering expertise and community

Developing internal experts in change management strengthens the organization’s ability to manage transitions smoothly. These specialists provide guidance and support, facilitating solutions to emerging challenges. Establishing networks for [knowledge exchange](https://www.glean.com/blog/enterprise-knowledge-management-guide) promotes shared learning and collective problem-solving.

By embedding a robust change management framework, organizations enhance their ability to respond to evolving demands. This capability not only strengthens competitive positioning but also ensures long-term resilience and adaptability.

## Future of change management

The future of change management is being transformed by advancements in [AI and analytics](https://www.glean.com/blog/enterprise-genai-guide-2024), which facilitate more tailored and proactive approaches. These technologies enable organizations to anticipate potential disruptions, allowing for more strategic planning and execution. By harnessing these insights, companies can refine processes in real time to maintain alignment with evolving objectives.

Organizations are increasingly embracing a mindset of continuous transformation, moving away from isolated change efforts. This shift promotes ongoing adaptability and resilience, positioning companies to swiftly seize new opportunities. The integration of agile principles with established change management practices supports this continuous evolution, fostering a culture of flexibility and innovation.

A major emphasis is now placed on enhancing the employee journey during transformations. Recognizing the importance of mental health and supportive environments, organizations are creating conditions conducive to successful change. This strategic shift underscores the role of leaders in fostering an environment where teams can navigate transitions with confidence and clarity.

The integration of digital tools streamlines change management processes, facilitating [communication and collaboration](https://www.glean.com/blog/glean-microsoft-teams). These tools empower teams to engage actively with change initiatives, enhancing overall effectiveness. As organizations increasingly prioritize strategic decision-making, they are adopting more collaborative approaches that empower employees at all levels to contribute to the change process.

As organizations continue to navigate an increasingly complex business landscape, the ability to manage change effectively will determine which companies thrive and which fall behind. The frameworks and strategies outlined here provide a foundation, but success ultimately depends on your organization's commitment to building a culture that embraces transformation as an ongoing journey rather than a destination.
