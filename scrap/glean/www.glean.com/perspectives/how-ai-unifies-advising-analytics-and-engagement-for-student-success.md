# How AI unifies advising, analytics, and engagement for student success

Higher education institutions collect vast amounts of student data across multiple systems, yet most struggle to transform this information into actionable insights that improve outcomes. The disconnect between academic records, engagement metrics, and support services creates blind spots where at-risk students fall through the cracks — often discovered too late for meaningful intervention.

Traditional approaches to student success rely on fragmented tools and reactive measures: advisors work from incomplete pictures, analytics remain trapped in departmental silos, and engagement efforts lack the personalization today's students expect. This fragmentation costs institutions millions in lost tuition revenue while failing the very students who need support most.

The convergence of artificial intelligence, cloud computing, and integrated data architectures now makes it possible to create a unified framework that connects every touchpoint of the student journey. This technological evolution promises to transform how institutions identify struggling students, deliver personalized support, and ultimately ensure more learners reach graduation.

## What is the student success layer?

The student success layer represents a fundamental shift in how educational institutions approach student support — moving from isolated interventions to an interconnected ecosystem powered by artificial intelligence. At its core, this framework creates a unified data environment where information flows seamlessly between academic systems, support services, and engagement platforms, eliminating the traditional barriers that have long plagued higher education.

Unlike conventional approaches that treat advising, analytics, and engagement as separate functions, the student success layer acts as connective tissue that binds these critical components together. This integration enables institutions to maintain a comprehensive, real-time view of each student's academic journey, behavioral patterns, and support needs. When a student misses multiple classes, struggles with coursework, or disengages from campus activities, the system immediately recognizes these warning signs across all touchpoints — not just within individual departments.

### Key components of the student success layer

The architecture of an effective student success layer rests on three foundational pillars:

-   **Unified data infrastructure**: A centralized repository that aggregates information from learning management systems, student information systems, campus card swipes, library usage, and other institutional databases. This creates a [single source of truth](https://www.glean.com/blog/enterprise-ai-knowledge-graph) that eliminates data inconsistencies and enables cross-functional analysis.
    
-   **AI-powered intelligence engine**: Machine learning algorithms that continuously analyze student data to identify patterns, predict outcomes, and generate actionable insights. These models adapt to each institution's unique student population and success factors, becoming more accurate over time.
    
-   Integrated intervention framework: Automated workflows that trigger appropriate support mechanisms based on AI insights — from personalized nudges to students to alerts for advisors when human intervention is needed. Oklahoma State University's randomized controlled [trial](https://www.insidehighered.com/news/students/retention/2025/01/22/research-shows-academic-alerts-impact-grades-withdrawals) found that students receiving academic alerts were 30% less likely to withdraw from courses and 4% more likely to receive grades higher than C compared to control groups. The alerts were most effective when they included specific information about the concern, why it mattered, and concrete steps the student could take. This ensures timely, coordinated responses across all support services.
    

### Breaking down institutional silos

Traditional higher education structures often create information silos where valuable data remains trapped within individual departments. The registrar's office holds enrollment data, academic departments track grades and attendance, student affairs monitors engagement activities, and financial aid manages economic indicators — yet these systems rarely communicate effectively.

The student success layer dissolves these boundaries through sophisticated [API integrations](https://www.glean.com/blog/open-agent-platform) and data standardization protocols. When implemented effectively, it enables scenarios where:

-   Academic advisors instantly see how a student's financial stress might be impacting their course performance
-   Residence life staff receive alerts when students in their halls show early signs of academic struggle
-   Career services can identify students whose academic choices misalign with their stated career goals
-   Faculty members gain visibility into which students might benefit from additional support based on engagement patterns across campus

This holistic view transforms how institutions understand and support their students, moving from reactive firefighting to proactive cultivation of success.

## How AI transforms academic advising

Artificial intelligence is reshaping academic advising by offering data-driven insights that guide students through their educational paths. By leveraging extensive data on course performance, engagement levels, and career objectives, AI develops tailored academic strategies for each student. This approach empowers students to make informed decisions, aligning their studies with long-term goals.

### The role of automated tools

Conversational AI enhances the advising experience by offering instant, round-the-clock assistance. These AI-driven advisors handle routine inquiries, freeing up human advisors to tackle more complex issues. Machine learning further personalizes this support by analyzing interaction patterns, ensuring students receive timely and relevant guidance when complexities arise. For example, Georgia State University's graduating class of 2016 saved [$18 million](https://success.gsu.edu/approach/) in cumulative tuition and fees compared to the 2012 class due to AI-driven interventions that reduced average time to degree by more than half a semester. The system makes over 2,000 corrections per year when students register for courses that don't count toward their degree programs, catching these errors before classes begin.

## Leveraging predictive analytics for student success

Predictive analytics is transforming how institutions anticipate and address student needs. By examining historical data, machine learning models reveal patterns and indicators unique to each campus, enabling a tailored approach to student support. However, AI algorithms used to predict college success show significant [racial bias](https://www.brookings.edu/articles/using-ai-to-predict-student-success-in-higher-education/), with Black and Hispanic students incorrectly predicted to fail 20% and 21% of the time respectively when they actually graduated, compared to only 12% for white students and 6% for Asian students. Among students who didn't complete degrees, the AI estimated success probability at 61% for white students but only 32% for Hispanic students and 39% for Black students in similar circumstances.

### Continuous monitoring and adaptive risk management

Through continuous monitoring of academic and engagement metrics, institutions gain insights into student behaviors and performance. This real-time data collection feeds into predictive models that assess the likelihood of student persistence, allowing educators to respond to emerging challenges promptly.

### Proactive strategies and targeted interventions

AI offers innovative ways to enhance student engagement by delivering personalized experiences tailored to individual needs. By analyzing behaviors and preferences, AI crafts targeted messaging that resonates with students. For example, students with zero engagement touchpoints during Fall 2023 had a 78% retention rate to Fall 2024, while those with four or more engagements had a 92% retention rate—a 14 percentage point differential. At the University of Essex, systematic [engagement analytics](https://www.campusmind.org/student-engagement-analytics-participation-data-retention/) reduced withdrawal rates from 88% to 20% for students identified as having low engagement at week six. This approach fosters a deeper connection, making the educational journey more engaging.

### Personalized communication and timing

Advanced AI systems generate bespoke content, ensuring that each message aligns with students' personal interests and academic goals. Utilizing data-driven insights, AI identifies the ideal moments to communicate, maximizing the impact of each interaction.

### Integrated multi-channel approach

Engaging students requires reaching them on their preferred platforms. AI seamlessly coordinates outreach across multiple channels — including email, text, and app notifications — to ensure consistent and effective communication. This strategy enhances the overall student experience by providing timely and relevant touchpoints.

## Implementation strategies for institutions

Embarking on [AI integration](https://www.glean.com/blog/cio-integrating-genai-agents) in student success requires strategic planning and thoughtful execution. Begin by conducting a detailed data assessment to understand existing systems and identify any gaps. This foundational analysis ensures that future initiatives are built on a solid understanding of the current environment.

### Data management and connectivity

Implementing strong data management practices is crucial. Establish frameworks that ensure [privacy protection](https://www.glean.com/blog/what-is-ai-security) while enabling the use of innovative solutions. Develop seamless integration points that allow various systems to communicate effectively, creating a cohesive network that enhances institutional operations.

### Enhancing infrastructure flexibility

Invest in adaptable infrastructure that can accommodate future growth. By leveraging cloud-based technologies, institutions can manage increasing data volumes and remain responsive to technological advancements. This flexibility ensures that the institution is prepared for both current and future demands.

### Promoting interdepartmental collaboration

Encouraging collaboration across departments is vital for successful AI adoption. Open communication and shared objectives help dismantle silos, allowing all stakeholders to engage with and benefit from AI-driven insights. Through collective efforts, institutions can align their strategies with overarching educational goals.

## Preparing for the future of AI in higher education

With AI's growing influence, emerging technologies are reshaping how institutions enhance the student journey. These advancements are set to revolutionize educational experiences and operational efficiency.

### Advanced interactions and immersive experiences

[AI-driven interfaces](https://www.glean.com/blog/glean-chat-launch-announcement) are evolving to provide more intuitive and natural student interactions. Through sophisticated dialogue systems, students will receive tailored support, improving their ability to manage academic and administrative tasks seamlessly. These systems aim to reduce friction, enhancing accessibility and engagement.

Immersive technologies, such as virtual environments, are transforming how students explore campus resources. By integrating digital information with physical spaces, students can interact with their surroundings in new ways, from virtual campus tours to interactive learning modules that adapt to individual needs.

### Secure credentials and enhanced computation

Distributed ledger technologies offer secure solutions for verifying academic credentials. These systems create immutable records, simplifying the verification process and fostering trust across institutions. This approach promises to streamline the exchange of academic information and support collaborative educational efforts.

Cutting-edge computational technologies are set to redefine data analysis capabilities. With advanced processing power, these innovations will provide deeper insights into student performance and institutional trends, enabling more strategic decision-making and personalized educational experiences.

## Taking action: your roadmap to unified AI approach

To successfully implement a unified AI approach, start by mapping out your institution's technological landscape. Identify areas where systems overlap or could better connect to enhance data flow and operational efficiency. This foundation sets the stage for a cohesive integration strategy.

### Collaborative needs assessment

Involve key stakeholders from the outset to gather diverse perspectives on current challenges and future goals. Facilitate workshops or interviews to uncover specific departmental needs and aspirations. This engagement ensures AI initiatives are purpose-driven, aligning with the institution's broader mission.

### Strategic rollout and continuous improvement

Explore AI solutions that are specifically designed for educational environments, focusing on adaptability and security. Implement these solutions in stages, allowing room for feedback and refinement. This methodical approach supports continuous improvement, ensuring that the institution remains agile and responsive to new insights and technologies.

The future of student success lies not in isolated improvements but in creating unified systems that connect every aspect of the educational journey. By breaking down silos between advising, analytics, and engagement, institutions can finally deliver the personalized, proactive support that today's students need to thrive. The technology exists today to transform fragmented data into actionable insights that drive meaningful outcomes for every learner.
