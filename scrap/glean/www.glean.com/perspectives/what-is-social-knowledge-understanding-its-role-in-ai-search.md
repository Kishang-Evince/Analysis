# What is social knowledge? Understanding its role in AI search

The digital workplace generates vast amounts of knowledge every day through conversations, collaborations, and shared experiences that traditional search tools struggle to capture. While [80%](https://edgedelta.com/company/blog/what-percentage-of-data-is-unstructured) of enterprise data exists in unstructured formats, only 40% of data infrastructure investment targets unstructured data processing. This collective intelligence—formed through human interactions and contextual understanding—represents one of the most valuable yet underutilized assets in modern organizations. While enterprises have invested heavily in document management systems and knowledge bases, they often miss the rich insights embedded in how teams actually work together and solve problems.

Social knowledge encompasses the unwritten expertise, informal networks, and institutional memory that make organizations truly function. It includes the context behind decisions, the reasoning that led to specific solutions, and the collaborative insights that emerge when teams tackle complex challenges together. This type of knowledge exists not in static documents but in the dynamic relationships between people, their shared experiences, and the collective wisdom they develop through working side by side.

The rise of AI-powered search technologies now offers unprecedented opportunities to make this hidden knowledge discoverable and actionable. Social knowledge comprises approximately [85%](https://scholarworks.waldenu.edu/dissertations/10115/) of organizational knowledge, yet remains largely inaccessible through traditional search paradigms. By understanding natural language, recognizing patterns in human interactions, and connecting disparate pieces of information across enterprise systems, AI transforms how organizations can access and leverage their collective intelligence. This shift from keyword-based searches to context-aware discovery represents a fundamental change in how enterprises can harness the full potential of their workforce's accumulated wisdom.

## What is social knowledge?

Social knowledge represents the collective intelligence that emerges from human interactions, shared experiences, and collaborative problem-solving within organizations. Unlike traditional explicit knowledge stored neatly in databases or formal documents, social knowledge thrives in the connections between people—their conversations, contextual understanding, and the informal networks that drive real work forward. This dynamic form of knowledge encompasses everything from water cooler insights to the collective reasoning behind major strategic decisions.

At its core, social knowledge includes the tacit expertise that employees carry within them: the unwritten rules of how things actually get done, the informal mentorship relationships that accelerate learning, and the institutional memory that prevents organizations from repeating past mistakes. When a senior engineer knows exactly which colleague to consult for a specific technical challenge, or when a sales representative understands the unspoken client preferences that close deals—these represent social knowledge in action. It manifests in team discussions where diverse perspectives converge to solve complex problems, in the lessons learned from project post-mortems that never make it into formal reports, and in the accumulated wisdom that develops through years of shared work experiences.

The power of social knowledge lies in its dual nature: it exists both as individual expertise distributed across an organization and as emergent intelligence that arises when people collaborate. Consider how a product development team draws upon:

-   **Collective problem-solving patterns**: The team's shared understanding of which approaches work best for different types of challenges, developed through countless iterations and experiments
-   **Cross-functional insights**: Knowledge that emerges at the intersection of different departments, such as when engineering and customer support collaborate to identify root causes of user issues
-   **Historical context**: The accumulated understanding of why certain decisions were made, what alternatives were considered, and what environmental factors influenced outcomes
-   **Informal expertise networks**: The web of relationships that employees tap into when facing novel challenges, often crossing hierarchical and departmental boundaries
-   **Cultural intelligence**: The shared understanding of organizational values, communication styles, and decision-making processes that enable effective collaboration

This form of knowledge proves particularly valuable in scenarios where formal documentation falls short. When facing unprecedented challenges or navigating complex organizational changes, teams rely heavily on social knowledge to adapt and innovate. The collective understanding of "how we do things here" combined with diverse individual experiences creates a rich knowledge ecosystem that no single repository could fully capture.

Social knowledge also evolves continuously through daily interactions. Each project completed, each problem solved, and each lesson learned adds to the collective intelligence of the organization. This constant evolution makes social knowledge both incredibly valuable and notoriously difficult to capture through traditional knowledge management approaches—setting the stage for why AI-powered search capabilities have become essential for modern enterprises seeking to unlock the full potential of their collective wisdom.

## How social knowledge differs from traditional knowledge

Traditional knowledge resides in structured formats—documents, databases, and organized [knowledge management systems](https://www.glean.com/blog/enterprise-knowledge-management-guide). It relies on explicit, codified information stored in clear hierarchies and taxonomies, making it easily searchable through keyword matching and predefined categories. This form of knowledge serves as a reliable reference for established procedures, standard operating protocols, and documented best practices.

In contrast, social knowledge emerges from the ongoing flow of human interactions and collective problem-solving. It is inherently flexible, shaped by the continuous exchange of ideas and experiences within teams. Unlike traditional knowledge, which remains stable and fixed, social knowledge captures the subtle insights derived from real-time collaboration and shared understanding.

### Key differences

-   **Evolving insights**: Social knowledge grows with each interaction, adapting as people collaborate and learn from one another.
    
-   **Deeper context**: While traditional knowledge focuses on explicit facts, social knowledge provides deeper insights into decision-making processes and cultural nuances that influence choices.
    
-   **Interconnected networks**: Social knowledge relies on the web of connections between people and ideas, going beyond the rigid structure of traditional systems.
    

Incorporating social knowledge into [enterprise search](https://www.glean.com/blog/what-is-enterprise-search) requires advanced AI capabilities to understand natural language, recognize patterns, and connect disparate information across systems. This approach—like we offer at Glean—enables organizations to harness the full potential of both traditional and social knowledge sources.

This model underscores the need for structured approaches to transform personal experiences into shared assets that benefit the entire organization.

In today's fast-paced environment, businesses must contend with the rapid creation of new knowledge while older information quickly loses relevance. Social learning becomes a strategic asset in this context, allowing organizations to adapt and thrive by drawing on their collective experiences. With the advent of digital transformation, opportunities to capture and share these insights have expanded through innovative collaboration platforms and integrated workspaces.

Moving from a reliance on individual expertise to fostering a culture of collective learning signifies a profound shift in maintaining competitive edge. By embracing this approach, companies can access a rich reservoir of experiences and innovative ideas within their teams, fostering an environment of continuous growth and adaptation crucial for success in the modern business landscape.

## Challenges in implementing social knowledge search

Implementing social knowledge search within an enterprise involves addressing several key challenges. Ensuring the integrity and coherence of information from diverse, unstructured sources is critical. Organizations must establish advanced data validation processes to maintain the reliability of information accessed through AI search systems. This involves deploying technologies that continuously monitor data for accuracy, ensuring that only credible insights enter the system.

Balancing security with accessibility is another critical challenge. Enterprises need to implement adaptive access protocols that prioritize both protection and ease of access. This requires creating intelligent systems capable of adjusting permissions based on user context and necessity, thereby facilitating secure yet comprehensive knowledge discovery.

Assessing the impact of social knowledge initiatives presents its own set of challenges. Organizations must define and regularly evaluate metrics that reflect the influence of AI-powered search on organizational effectiveness and innovation. These metrics ensure that initiatives remain aligned with strategic business goals.

Addressing privacy concerns while capturing collaborative insights requires transparency and robust safeguards. It is essential to implement comprehensive privacy measures that protect data while fostering trust among employees. Managing the complexity of different knowledge formats and languages demands that AI models are tailored to understand specific organizational contexts and terminologies, ensuring that search results are relevant and accurate.

## Building an effective social knowledge search strategy

### Establish strong foundations

Begin by evaluating your existing knowledge assets to uncover areas for improvement. This assessment helps clarify the organization's knowledge landscape, setting the stage for strategic enhancements.

Set specific goals for social knowledge initiatives that align with business objectives. This alignment ensures efforts directly support organizational priorities. Establish frameworks that facilitate knowledge sharing while maintaining robust security protocols, creating an environment where information flows securely and effectively.

Develop clear metrics to assess how social knowledge search impacts productivity and innovation. These metrics guide improvements and highlight areas needing attention. Encourage a culture that prioritizes open dialogue and continuous learning, empowering employees to actively engage in the organization's knowledge ecosystem.

### Implement AI search capabilities strategically

Start by identifying impactful use cases for AI search to address immediate organizational needs. Demonstrating tangible benefits early on fosters broader adoption. Ensure seamless integration with existing tools to provide a consistent user experience.

Design intuitive interfaces to facilitate easy adoption and minimize training requirements. User-friendly designs help employees transition smoothly to new systems. Establish feedback mechanisms to refine search relevance and accuracy continuously, adapting to changing needs.

Scale initiatives gradually, maintaining high standards of quality and user satisfaction. This approach ensures sustainable growth aligned with user expectations. Monitor patterns of use to discover new opportunities for knowledge exploration and innovation.

Invest in change management to ensure a smooth transition and maximize the value of new technologies. Preparing teams for changes in knowledge access and utilization supports successful AI integration into daily workflows. This strategic implementation positions organizations to leverage collective intelligence effectively, driving innovation and efficiency across all operations.
