## FAQs

How can financial institutions reduce time spent searching across policies, client records, research, and operational systems?

Financial institutions can reduce search time by connecting the knowledge teams rely on every day: policies, client records, research, market context, and operational data. With the right AI platform, employees can find trusted answers and assemble the context they need without jumping between systems. That helps teams prepare faster, serve clients more effectively, and make decisions with greater confidence across client service, risk, compliance, and operations.

What does a secure AI rollout in financial services require beyond a polished chatbot demo?

A secure rollout needs the same things that make AI useful in production: permission-aware access, governance, trusted data, secure integrations, and workflows that fit the way teams already work. A demo can show what the experience feels like. A production rollout has to prove AI can support service, compliance, operations, and advisory work without creating new risk or another disconnected tool.

How can banks keep AI-generated answers aligned with current policies, approved guidance, and the latest regulatory changes?

Banks need AI grounded in trusted, current sources, including policy documents, approved guidance, audit materials, prior findings, and regulatory updates. Answers should reflect the permissions and sources available at the time of the request, with clear links back to source material so teams can verify what they’re using. That helps employees respond faster to regulatory change without relying on outdated files or fragmented internal knowledge.

What should banks evaluate if they already use Microsoft 365 Copilot but still need access to information outside Microsoft 365?

Banks should evaluate whether employees can use AI across the systems where work actually happens, not just inside Microsoft 365. Policy, client, operational, and research context often lives in Google Drive, Box, Confluence, Jira, ServiceNow, Salesforce, Workday, and other business systems. The key question is whether teams can bring that context into one trusted, permission-aware experience.

How do permission-aware search, real-time access controls, and auditability work in a regulated financial environment?

Permission-aware search means AI only uses and shows information an employee is already allowed to access. If access changes, answers should reflect those changes. Auditability gives teams a clear view into which sources supported an answer, making it easier to review, verify, and respond to compliance or regulatory questions. Together, these controls help financial institutions use AI without loosening the safeguards around sensitive information.

How can banks give advisors a complete view of research, client preferences, and product rules without forcing them to switch between disconnected tools?

Banks can give advisors a more complete view by connecting research, client history, product rules, and internal guidance in one secure, permission-aware system. Instead of switching between tools before every client conversation, advisors can quickly create briefs and prep materials grounded in the latest available context. That helps them deliver more personalized guidance while staying aligned to approved policies and product rules.

How can commercial and relationship teams walk into deal and client meetings with pitch materials, client history, market insights, and compliance records already in one place?

Commercial and relationship teams can use AI to gather pitch materials, client history, market insights, and compliance records before a meeting and turn them into meeting-ready briefs. With the right context in one place, teams can walk in prepared, tailor the conversation to the client, and reduce the manual work required to assemble materials.

How can banks improve AI adoption quickly by fitting search, assistants, and agents into the workflows teams already use every day?

Banks improve adoption when AI fits into the tools and workflows employees already use. Search, assistants, and agents should make everyday work easier, whether teams are preparing for client meetings, finding policy guidance, resolving service issues, or completing operational tasks. When AI is useful in the work people already do, it’s easier to move from pilot to business impact.
